# AVMC AI Use, Attribution, and Verification Notes

**Appalachian Valley Medical Center**  
**Cybersecurity Capstone - Week 8**  
**Student:** James Jordon  
**AI System Used:** OpenAI ChatGPT - GPT-5.6 Sol  
**Purpose:** Drafting assistance, technical discussion, documentation organization, and review

---

## 1. Purpose of This Disclosure

Artificial intelligence was used during portions of the AVMC cybersecurity capstone as a drafting, organizational, troubleshooting, and learning aid. AI-generated material was not treated as an authoritative cybersecurity source. Technical claims, security-framework mappings, and incident-response recommendations were reviewed against authoritative sources including NIST, CISA, and SANS materials.

The AVMC Packet Tracer environment, configurations, troubleshooting, testing, screenshots, incident-response decisions, and final design choices were performed, reviewed, or approved by the student. No real patient information, protected health information (PHI), organizational credentials, or real company data were entered into the AI system. AVMC is a fictional healthcare organization created for educational purposes.

## 2. AI System Attribution

> **OpenAI. ChatGPT (GPT-5.6 Sol) [Large language model]. Used October 2026.**

ChatGPT was used interactively for documentation organization, networking and cybersecurity discussion, Packet Tracer configuration guidance, interpretation of test results, policy drafting assistance, NIST CSF 2.0 mapping organization, tabletop structure, professionalization of student decisions, GitHub-ready Markdown, evidence organization, and review. AI output was reviewed before incorporation into final artifacts.

## 3. Work Performed by the Student

The student remained responsible for the AVMC network design and final decisions, including building and modifying the Packet Tracer topology; configuring VLANs, SVIs, addressing, DHCP, DNS, endpoints, ACLs, and security zones; performing connectivity tests; interpreting simulator behavior; troubleshooting; verifying ACL counters; creating and testing AVMC web services; conducting ransomware-tabletop decisions; identifying the missing isolated backup tier; implementing VLAN 90 and `COLD-BACKUP1`; capturing evidence; and reviewing final documentation. The AI system did not directly operate Cisco Packet Tracer.

## 4. Security Policy Drafting

AI assistance was used to help draft the required Acceptable Use, Access Control, and Incident Response policies. The policies were written for the fictional AVMC environment rather than copied directly from a generic template. The student reviewed and approved the final substantive language.

## 5. Authoritative Verification Sources

Primary verification sources included NIST Cybersecurity Framework 2.0, NIST SP 800-61 Revision 3, CISA tabletop and ransomware guidance, and SANS security-policy resources. These sources were used to check governance, protection, access control, incident management, containment, recovery, improvement, and policy concepts.

## 6. AI Recommendations That Were Reviewed or Modified

AI recommendations were not automatically accepted. Examples included correcting an initial physical-ransom-note interpretation after the scenario specified a text file; refining containment to avoid unnecessarily shutting down the entire hospital network; distinguishing law-enforcement coordination from HIPAA breach-notification analysis; documenting Packet Tracer's limitations rather than claiming true immutable storage; and recognizing through testing that VLAN separation alone did not provide recovery isolation when Layer 3 routing remained available.

## 7. Student Decision-Making During the Ransomware Tabletop

The student made the initial response decisions for each inject before those decisions were organized into the final After-Action Report. Decisions included isolating affected systems, preserving evidence, expanding containment, investigating the phishing vector, reviewing potentially compromised accounts, escalating to leadership/legal/privacy/cybersecurity/communications personnel, recommending a limited factual public statement, recommending against ransom payment, continuing investigation after confirmed exfiltration, and identifying the need for an isolated recovery tier.

## 8. AI Assistance With Technical Configuration

AI provided configuration suggestions during portions of the Packet Tracer build. Suggested commands were reviewed, entered by the student, observed in Cisco IOS/Packet Tracer output, functionally tested, and modified when results did not match the intended design. ACL match counters and connectivity tests were used as evidence rather than assuming generated commands were correct.

## 9. AI Assistance With Documentation

AI assisted with structuring prose, organizing screenshots, creating evidence filenames, drafting README files, generating Markdown versions, improving formatting, organizing the After-Action Report and Tool Evidence report, maintaining terminology, identifying missing documentation, and improving GitHub presentation. This assistance changed the presentation of the project but did not replace the underlying technical work or testing.

## 10. Attribution in Portfolio Artifacts

> **AI Assistance:** OpenAI ChatGPT (GPT-5.6 Sol) was used for drafting assistance, technical discussion, documentation organization, and review. AI-generated cybersecurity statements were reviewed against NIST, CISA, SANS, course materials, and observed Packet Tracer results before inclusion. Final network configurations, tests, incident-response decisions, edits, and conclusions were reviewed or performed by the student.

## 11. Data Privacy and Responsible AI Use

AVMC is fictional. The AI system was not provided with real patient records, PHI, medical records, production credentials, real customer records, or confidential employer information. Screenshots and configuration evidence were reviewed for portfolio suitability. Lab-only credentials are not intended for use in any real environment.

## 12. Limitations of AI Assistance

Generative AI can produce incorrect, incomplete, outdated, or inappropriate technical recommendations. AI output was treated as a starting point for review rather than as proof. Technical validation relied on Packet Tracer results, Cisco IOS output, connectivity tests, ACL hit counters, application behavior, authoritative cybersecurity references, and student review. Verified evidence took priority over AI suggestions.

## 13. Final Verification Statement

AI was used as an educational and documentation-support tool. The student remained responsible for reviewing, testing, correcting, accepting, or rejecting AI-generated recommendations. Security policies were checked against NIST and SANS resources; incident-response concepts were reviewed against NIST SP 800-61 Rev. 3, NIST CSF 2.0, and relevant CISA guidance; and technical recommendations were validated through the Packet Tracer environment whenever supported.
